# 1. CORE PRINCIPLE

BidShield is a statistical screening system.

It does NOT determine that collusion, bid rigging, fraud, corruption, or wrongdoing occurred.

Every result must be phrased as:

* "Statistical indicator"
* "Potentially unusual pattern"
* "Requires further review"
* "Observed bidding pattern"

Never use:

* "Collusion detected"
* "Fraud detected"
* "Corrupt company"
* "Confirmed bid rigging"
* "Criminal bidder"

The application must display:

> "Statistical screening identifies patterns that may warrant further review. It does not establish collusion, wrongdoing, or legal liability."

This should appear on relevant screening pages and investigation pages.

# 5. WINNER ROTATION SCREEN

Implement a winner-rotation screening engine.

The purpose is to identify unusually structured patterns in which a recurring group of bidders appears across comparable tenders and different members repeatedly win.

IMPORTANT:

Do not assume that repeated winners automatically imply coordination.

Legitimate factors such as capability, geography, specialization, pricing, or qualification can produce repeated patterns.

The detector should identify patterns for review.

---

# 6. WINNER ROTATION — DATA PREPARATION

Group tenders by relevant comparison dimensions where available:

* organization
* department
* category
* location
* procurement type
* time period

Do not compare unrelated tenders blindly.

For each group:

1. identify participating companies
2. identify winner
3. order tenders chronologically
4. create winner sequences

Example:

Tender A → Company 1
Tender B → Company 2
Tender C → Company 3
Tender D → Company 1
Tender E → Company 2

This is a pattern that may warrant investigation.

---

# 7. WINNER ROTATION METRICS

Implement measurable metrics rather than a hardcoded "rotation detected" flag.

Possible metrics:

### Winner concentration

Measure how concentrated wins are among participating firms.

### Winner transition pattern

Analyze transitions:

A → B
B → C
C → A

### Alternation frequency

Measure how often a small recurring group takes turns winning.

### Repeated participant set

Measure how frequently the same group of companies participates together.

### Sequence regularity

Measure whether winner sequences exhibit unusual regularity relative to an appropriate baseline.

Do not claim that a particular statistical threshold proves collusion.

---

# 8. ROTATION SCORE

Create an initial normalized indicator:

`rotation_score`

Range:

0–100

Higher values indicate stronger statistical indicators of structured winner patterns.

The score must be explainable.

Do NOT simply generate random scores.

Store the underlying metrics.

Example:

rotation_score:
78

Supporting metrics:

winner_concentration:
0.72

alternation_frequency:
0.81

repeated_group_rate:
0.76

sequence_regularity:
0.69

The exact weighting should be documented in code and README.

Make weights configurable.

# 9. WINNER ROTATION EVIDENCE

For every flagged tender/group, generate evidence.

Example:

> "Within the selected comparison group, 4 companies participated in 17 tenders. Wins alternated among the same group across 11 of those tenders."

Another:

> "The current tender belongs to a group where the same set of bidders has repeatedly appeared together."

Do not say:

> "These companies are colluding."

Evidence must be based on actual calculated metrics.

Store evidence in the existing `evidence` table.

Use:

indicator_type:

`WINNER_ROTATION`

# 10. COVER-BID SCREENING

Implement a statistical screen for bids that appear unusually positioned relative to the winning bid.

The goal is to identify suspiciously structured bid relationships for further review.

Do not assume every higher losing bid is a cover bid.

---

# 11. COVER-BID METRICS

For each tender:

Calculate:

### Relative bid distance

For each losing bid:

`(losing_bid - winning_bid) / winning_bid`

### Bid spread

Measure the spread among submitted bids.

### Losing-bid position

Analyze how losing bidders are positioned relative to the winner.

### Historical bidder behavior

Compare a company's bid positioning across comparable tenders.

### Repeated proximity pattern

Identify bidders that repeatedly submit bids at similar relative distances from the winner.

---

# 12. COVER-BID SCREENING EXAMPLE

Suppose:

Winner:

₹10,00,000

Other bids:

₹10,80,000
₹10,82,000
₹10,85,000

Calculate:

8.0%
8.2%
8.5%

If similar positioning occurs repeatedly for the same bidders across comparable tenders, increase the screening indicator.

Do not use a single tender as sufficient evidence.

Historical repetition should matter.

---

# 13. COVER-BID SCORE

Create:

`cover_bid_score`

Range:

0–100

It should consider:

* relative bid distance
* consistency
* historical repetition
* bidder-specific behavior
* comparable tender behavior

Document the methodology.

# 14. COVER-BID EVIDENCE

Example:

> "Company B submitted a bid 8.1% above the winning bid in 7 comparable tenders. Its median relative distance from the winner in this category is 8.0%."

This is evidence.

Do NOT write:

> "Company B submitted cover bids."

Instead:

> "Company B shows a repeated bid-positioning pattern that warrants review."

Store:

indicator_type:

`COVER_BID`
